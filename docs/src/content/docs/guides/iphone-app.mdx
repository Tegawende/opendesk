The opendesk iPhone app is a native app for your daily work: tickets and live chat, Collab mail, contacts, the knowledge base, the wiki, reports and Ollie. It needs iOS 26 or later and works with opendesk Cloud and with self-hosted instances.

## Get started

<Steps>
<Step title="Connect to a server">

When you open the app, it asks which server to use.

- **opendesk Cloud:** type `opendesk.sh`.
- **Your own server:** type its address, such as `desk.example.com`.

The app reads the server's `/config.json` to find its API, then asks the server how you can sign in. A self-hosted server needs a release that serves `/config.json`.

</Step>
<Step title="Sign in">

Use whichever way your server offers:

| Option | Cloud | Self-hosted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email and password, with your two-factor code | Yes | Yes |
| Google, Microsoft, GitHub or SSO | Yes, those the server offers | Yes, those the server offers |
| Passkeys | Yes | No |

**Google, Microsoft, GitHub and SSO** open in the system sign-in sheet, the same one Safari uses, and return to the app when you are done. If your account has a password and you sign in with a provider for the first time, the app asks for the password once to link the two.

</Step>
<Step title="Allow notifications">

When the app asks, allow notifications. Assigned to you, mentioned, customer replied and SLA breached then reach the phone as push notifications. Tap one to open its ticket.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="note" title="Passkeys work on Cloud only">

A passkey belongs to a website's domain, and iOS lets an app use it only for domains that the app lists when it is built and that list the app in return. The App Store app lists `app.opendesk.sh`. It cannot list every self-hosted domain, so on your own server sign in with a password, a provider or SSO.

</Callout>

## Push notifications

The app icon's badge counts your unread notifications across all your workspaces and clears as you read them, on any device.

- **Cloud:** push works out of the box. Notifications show the ticket subject.
- **Self-hosted:** push depends on how the server is set up, and may be off. Notifications sent through the opendesk relay show the type and ticket number but not the subject, which never leaves your server. See [Push notifications](/self-hosting/push-notifications/).

In the app's notification settings you can turn off each type for this phone, see the other phones signed in to your account, and remove one. When the server has push off, the settings say so; the app still updates live while it is open.

<Callout>

Signing out, changing your password, or signing the session out from another device ends the phone's session, and it stops receiving notifications at once. Your account's session list shows it as **iPhone**.

</Callout>

## On the phone and on the web

The app does what the web app does, with a few exceptions:

| On the phone | Opens the web app inside the app |
| --- | --- |
| Inbox: folders, views, team inboxes, filters, sorting, bulk edits | Email design |
| Spaces, and the Everywhere lists across all your workspaces | Importing from another helpdesk |
| Search across tickets, contacts and articles, or ask Ollie | Signing in to Google or Microsoft for a mailbox or a mail account |
| New tickets, ticket view, details, custom fields and live chat | Signing in to an integration that uses its own sign-in |
| Collab mail, contacts, companies, the knowledge base, the wiki and reports | Formatted signatures, image uploads for brands and widgets, and the widget preview |
| Your profile, security, notifications, saved replies, views and agent-to-agent | |
| Every workspace setting, from members and teams to mailboxes, automations, the AI agent, API keys and the audit log | |

Workspace settings are under **Account**, grouped as on the web. Everyone can read them; only admins change them. Billing shows your plan, seats and usage, and is changed on the web. Pages that open the web app use a browser inside the app, which keeps its own web session, so it may ask you to sign in there once.

## The wiki

The **Wiki** on the phone has the same sections as on the web: favorites, your teamspaces, the workspace's pages, your private pages and recent ones, and the trash.

- Read and write pages, add subpages, star favorites and move pages to the trash, with **Undo**. Restore them from the trash.
- See which pages are verified, follow pages, and read and write comment threads.
- Open a database's rows as a list, each row as its page.
- Pages are written as Markdown on the phone. Blocks made on the web, such as databases, embeds and ticket lists, show as links and cards; a page that holds them opens for editing as HTML, so nothing in it is lost. **Edit as HTML**, in the editor's menu, opens any page that way.

Database views and property values, and comments on a run of text, are on the web and the [desktop app](/guides/desktop-app/). Wiki mentions, comments and reminders arrive as push notifications when **Phone** is on for them. See [Wiki](/guides/wiki/).

## Several accounts

Under your profile on **Account**, choose **Add another account** to sign in to a second account, on opendesk Cloud or another server, without signing out of the first. Tap an account to switch to it, or touch and hold the **Account** tab to switch from anywhere. The app holds up to five accounts.

A notification for another account opens that account when you tap it. On one server, the phone gets notifications for the account you opened there most recently.

## Security

- Provider and SSO sign-ins are protected with PKCE, so only the app that started the sign-in can finish it.
- Where the web asks for a Turnstile check, the app proves instead that it is the genuine opendesk app on a genuine iPhone, with Apple's App Attest. You see nothing. On a self-hosted server with Turnstile on, the administrator sets `APP_ATTEST_APP_IDS` so the server accepts that proof (see [Configuration reference](/self-hosting/configuration/#native-apps)).
- The app keeps its session in the iPhone's Keychain.

</Fold>
